# Hydrostatic balance at domain walls

Diagnosis and fix for the time-evolved pressure error of the `hydrostatic`
fixture, which starts from the exact equation-of-state profile and drifted to a
30% relative error at the floor corners. Baseline `a378fc8`; the manuscript in
`docs/formal/` describes the pre-fix scheme and its archived data are not
regenerated by this change.

Executable evidence: `core/tests/hydrostatic_balance.rs` (fourteen native tests,
including the matched-resolution study) and `core/tests/wall_friction.rs`
(five Coulomb/unilateral tests), all included in `make gate`. The kernel tables below come from
`Simulation::advance_to` in the Rust core driven directly by `cargo test`, not
through the Python CLI; nothing is mocked and no fixture is special-cased.
Independent compiled CLI runs subsequently reproduced the original and six-case
hydrostatic study values; see `validation.md`. A longer native run at `0.1 s`
(2000 steps) gives max `0.012738803387135723`, rms `0.0038023664104390336`.

## Symptom

Water column, `rho = 1000`, `E = 1e5`, `nu = 0.2`, `mu = 1e-3`, `h = 2 mm`,
`g = 9.81`, wall friction `0.4`, domain `20 x 20 x 20 mm`, column
`20 x 20 x 10 mm`, `dt = 5e-5`, `0.01 s` (4000 particles, 200 steps, zero
limited or rejected steps, mass and momentum residuals at roundoff). Error is
`|p - p_exact(z)| / (rho g H)` over every particle.

| case | max | rms |
| --- | --- | --- |
| original, `0.01 s`, before | `0.29698913693243917` | `0.06463322509348367` |
| original, `0.01 s`, after | `0.011513856585915002` | `0.0034430221485745437` |
| `4 x 4 mm` footprint (160 particles), `0.002 s`, before | `0.05806033570144581` | `0.017619387677450565` |
| same, after | `0.004780974064310695` | `0.00106261024765249` |

The `4 x 4 mm` footprint is two cells wide, so its side-wall nodes see a
symmetric particle lattice and only the floor truncation acts. That is why the
reduced case was three times better than the original: the original's corners
stack a floor and two side-wall errors.

## Root cause

First step from rest with the exact prestress, accelerations of free grid nodes
in units of `g` (`first_step_from_exact_profile_is_balanced_at_every_free_node`,
before the fix):

| node | `a / g` |
| --- | --- |
| interior (two or more cells from every wall, submerged) | `~1e-9` |
| one cell above the floor, `z = dx` | `-0.4006` (vertical) |
| one cell inside a side wall, `x = dx`, at `p_i = 4 rho g dx` | `-0.3157` (horizontal, toward the wall) |
| same node at `p_i = 2 rho g dx` | `-0.1587` |
| floor corner `(dx, dx, dx)` | `(-0.316, -0.316, -0.401)`, `|a| = 0.60` |
| one cell below the free surface | `-0.004` |

These match a closed-form calculation on the two-particle-per-cell lattice with
quadratic B-splines. The MLS-MPM internal force

```
f_i = (4 / dx^2) sum_p V_p p_p w_ip (x_i - x_p)
```

is a quadrature surrogate of the weak-form term `-∫ σ:∇N_i dV`: it replaces
`∇N_i(x_p)` by `(4 / dx^2) w_ip (x_i - x_p)` and integrates over particles. For
a linear pressure `p_p = p_i - grad p . (x_i - x_p)` the node force splits into
`p_i * M1` plus gradient terms, with the first moment
`M1 = sum_p V_p w_ip (x_i - x_p)`. On a symmetric lattice `M1 = 0` and the
second moment `sum_p V_p w_ip (x_i - x_p)^2 = V_p dx^2 / 2` balances `m_i g`
exactly (weights `0.6875, 0.28125, 0.03125` at `0.25, 0.75, 1.25 dx`). One
cell inside a wall the lattice column at `-0.25 dx` is missing, so
`M1 = -0.0390625 V_p dx` along the truncated axis and the *zeroth order*
pressure pulls the node toward the wall:

- floor node: `[-0.15625 p_i / dx - 0.1640625 rho g] / (1.96875 rho)` with
  `p_i = 4 rho g dx` gives `-0.4008 g` (measured `-0.4006`, the rest is EOS
  curvature)
- side node: `a = -0.0794 p_i / (rho dx)`. At fixed physical depth this
  acceleration grows as `1 / dx` under refinement, because a fixed local
  pressure acts over a shrinking cell. What refinement does not shrink is the
  *equilibrated* pressure error: the first cell has to compress until its
  pressure rise cancels the pull, and the required rise is a fixed fraction
  (`~0.1`) of the local pressure, independent of `dx`. Only the layer
  thickness shrinks, so the max-norm error would not converge

In the continuum weak form the corresponding term is the face integral
`p ∮ N_i n dA`: the internal force of a node whose support crosses the boundary
is `∫ N_i div(σ) dV - ∫_Γ N_i σ n dA`, and the momentum equation needs the wall
traction `∫_Γ N_i (σ n) dA` to cancel it. The node-wise projection constrains
only nodes at or behind the wall plane and never supplies that traction to the
first free node. The discrete surrogate is not the continuum integral, though:
on the lattice the deficit is `0.0769 p dx^2` per truncated axis at a corner
node while the exact face integral is `0.123 p dx^2`, so adding the continuum
traction would over-correct the MLS node by 60%. The repair has to be stated in
the surrogate's own terms, as a lattice completion.

The pressure in the first cell rises until the discrete system balances, about
`0.5 rho g dx` at the floor and `0.1 p_local` at a side wall (predicted
`0.103 rho g H` for the reduced case, measured `0.108` at `0.01 s`). This is a
consequence of the node-wise wall projection with this MLS quadrature, not a
stencil-indexing bug; the tested wall layers carried errors of order 10%.

Falsified: temporal integration (H3). Halving `dt` from `5e-5` to `1.25e-5`
moved the pre-fix error by a few percent only, and the first-step acceleration
is independent of `dt` by construction. APIC amplification (H2) is a
consequence, not a cause: the affine matrix of the near-wall particles picks
up the compression once the node moves.

## Fix: face-normal lattice completion on the active contact set

`Simulation::wall_traction_to_grid` (called at the end of `particle_to_grid`)
completes the truncated lattice one face at a time. Every particle closer than
`dx / 2` to a wall face (the only distance at which its mirror image reaches a
free node) emits one image mirrored in that face alone. The image deposits
only the face-normal component of its MLS stress force plus its weight:

```
Δp_i,n = w_ig [ -(4 dt / dx^2) (V σ_nn)_g (x_i - x_g)_n + m_p g_n dt ]
```

with `(V σ_nn)_g = V0 τ_nn - ρ V g_n (x_g - x_p)_n`: the particle's normal
stress continued across the face with the normal momentum balance of material
in contact with a stationary wall, `∂σ_nn/∂n = -ρ g_n` (`a_n = 0`). The following
rules define the contact treatment:

- Tensile continuation, nothing. A wall cannot pull, so if `(V σ_nn)_g >= 0`
  there is no image material along that face and no image weight either. The
  weight is part of the continued equilibrium field, not a separate load;
  keeping it while dropping the stress pushed a stress-free body away from a
  wall that gravity pointed away from.
- Active set from the wall-plane node. The image force is the reaction of the
  node-wise constraint carried to the first free node, so it shares that
  constraint's active set. An image mirrored in axis `a` only reaches the
  first free node along `a`; its wall-plane neighbour (same tangential
  index, padded `1` or `cells + 1`) is loaded by the same parent with the same
  tangential weights, so it has mass whenever the image weight is nonzero, and
  no image ever deposits an `a`-component on an `a`-wall node, so its trial
  normal velocity `p_i / m_i + g_a dt` is untouched by the traction pass. The
  deposit is made only if that trial velocity is inward, the same strict test
  `grid_update` uses to project the node. Separating material therefore
  receives nothing; a resting column is inward at every wall node (the wall
  node's own truncated stencil pulls it at several `g`), so hydrostatics stay
  active.
- Non-negative reaction only. The deposit for one image at one node is the
  continued stress force plus the image weight; when a vanishing continued
  stress meets gravity into the wall (`p ≈ 0`, `d < 0.11 dx`) that sum points
  toward the wall, and such an entry is skipped: a pulling image is not a
  wall reaction. Every booked reaction is therefore inward and the total
  normal reaction per node, projection plus images, is unilateral.
- Coulomb on the total reaction. Each booked image reaction is recorded on
  the receiving free node (`Grid::traction`, per axis) and `grid_update`
  applies the same Coulomb law used at wall nodes to that node's tangential
  plane with budget `μ N_image`, capped at stick, never reversing. The wall
  nodes keep their own budget from the projection. When every loaded node
  slides in one direction the total tangential impulse is exactly `μ` times
  the total normal impulse (`sliding_floor_impulse_obeys_coulomb_limit_for_total_normal_reaction`:
  ratio `1.000000000000`, baseline identical); without this the image
  reaction escaped the friction budget and the effective friction dropped by
  8% (`0.920684093960`).
- Nothing tangential is ever deposited by the images themselves, so a stress-free body falling or
  sliding past a wall receives exactly nothing and the tangential reaction
  stays with the Coulomb node projection. The component is also dropped at
  nodes that are wall nodes along that axis: there the projection is the
  reaction, and the padding node one cell behind the wall carries
  `w(1.25) = 1/32` of a particle mass against the image's `w(0.75)` momentum,
  which an earlier all-component variant turned into `~170 g` per step for
  the projection to erase.

Images only reach nodes their parent already loaded, so no massless node
receives momentum. The near-zero inward-trial test at `d/h = 0.05` checks that
a negative image-weight correction cannot overwhelm a tiny projection reaction.
Both reaction paths share the same Coulomb reduction. A zero friction budget
leaves even a `1e-16 m/s` tangent unchanged, rather than applying the old
`1e-14 m/s` cutoff (which incorrectly removed momentum when `mu = 0`).

Why single-face images suffice at corners: the `a`-force balance at a node
only involves `a`-components. At a corner node the `a`-force lattice is real
particles plus `a`-images, symmetric row by row along `a`, while the other
truncated axis scales the stress sum and the node mass by the same factor
(`1.96875 / 2`), so both sides of the balance shrink together. Measured
first-step accelerations one cell inside every wall, corners included, are
`7e-7 g` with the images alone and exactly `0` once the Coulomb budget sticks
that residual creep (`μ = 0.4`).

Why this is a nodal surrogate, not an exact boundary solution: the image
stress is the diagonal component extrapolated by the normal balance only. The
relation `grad p = ρ g` holds for a liquid at rest, but a linear extrapolation
still approximates its spatially varying density. In moving material it also
drops tangential shear gradients `∂σ_nt/∂t`. No general convergence order is
established for this boundary treatment. For stress-free
material approaching a wall the continuation still yields the half-cell layer
weight `2 d ρ g_n` as image stress before any real stress has built up; that
is a transient nodal estimate of the reaction, of order `0.02 g` on the first
free layer for one or two steps, not a physical traction. The true wall
reaction is a Lagrange multiplier of the contact constraint; this scheme
estimates it from the material's own normal stress, which is what continuity
of traction says at a contact, and leaves any residual to the projection.

The traction impulse is booked into `Ledger::wall_impulse`, so the momentum
residual stays at roundoff. The wall-contact milestone initially omitted its grid
KE jump. This is now booked as signed `wall_normal_traction_energy`, evaluated
at each normal deposit before gravity. It changes the algebraic energy residual,
not the trajectory or any numerical result below other than that residual.
It is not physical stationary-wall work or a closed energy balance; see
`energy-ledgers.md` for the substep definition and restart rules.

Two earlier variants and how they failed (both tests retained):

- All components of every image plus corner images, relying on the volume
  identity `∫_Γ N_i σ n dA = f^MLS(σ_g) + m_g g`, which needs
  `div(σ_g) = -ρ g` on the image region. A stress-free block falling parallel
  to frictionless side walls picked up `1.03e-3 m/s` per `5e-5 s` step
  (`free_fall_parallel_to_frictionless_sidewalls_has_no_extra_acceleration`).
- Face-normal images without an active set: a stress-free block on the floor
  moving upward at `0.3 m/s` received `8.16e-6 m/s` and a `9.2e-9 kg m/s`
  upward wall impulse from the contact continuation
  (`stress_free_material_separating_from_floor_has_no_wall_force`).

Both now give the baseline's values: `1.08e-19 m/s` and `2.78e-16 m/s`,
zero wall impulse.

Contact range, retained as a bounded test
(`subcell_gap_does_not_create_attraction_or_excess_contact_force`): the
compressed block with its lowest particle *cells* `0.15 dx` above the floor
(centres at `0.4 dx`; a touching lattice has centres at `dx / 4`) is treated
as contact. The wall-plane node carries mass and an approaching trial velocity
from the pressure gradient, so the baseline projection already reacts
`9.156e-7 kg m/s` on this state; the traction brings it to `9.311e-7`, both
under the full contact value `p A dt = 1.112e-6`. This is the pre-existing
support/contact range of the quadratic stencil (bodies within `1.5 dx` load
wall nodes), not a resolved physical contact distance. The test bounds the reaction between
zero and `p A dt`; it does not require this known premature contact to persist.
The separate release checks above use stress-free states.

## Remaining limitations

- Free surface. The residual `~0.6-1%` sits in the top two particle rows
  (under-pressure) and decays into the column: floor layer `2.5e-5`, two cells
  below the surface `1e-3`. The node just above the surface holds `1/32` of a
  particle mass under a one-sided stencil and starts at `+0.26 g`; the surface
  row's `rho g dx / 4` pressure has no lattice row above it. This traction-free
  truncation is self-consistent for momentum but first order in `dx / H` for
  the surface rows (`0.0098 -> 0.0062` from `2 mm` to `1 mm`) and also shrinks
  with `dt` (`0.0115 -> 0.0075` from `5e-5` to `1.25e-5`). It is not a wall
  effect. The rough side walls (`μ = 0.4`) now also stick the surface rows'
  slow vertical creep through the image budget, which is why these numbers
  moved by `~15%` relative when the Coulomb budget was added; that is the
  friction law acting, not a new accuracy claim. No convergence rate or
  closure is claimed from these six points. A separate 0.1-second test with
  **zero** wall friction has max error `0.010002518988766813`, so the
  improvement does not depend on Coulomb damping of the pressure error.
- Paste columns get only the per-axis continuation, not an exact boundary solution: `0.0080` max after `0.002 s`
  (`paste_column_pressure_error_stays_small_after_forty_steps`).
- Sub-half-cell gaps under an approaching wall node are treated as contact,
  as they already were by the projection (contact-range note above).
- Stress-free material approaching a wall gets the half-cell layer weight as
  image stress for the steps before real stress builds: a nodal surrogate of
  the reaction, `~0.02 g` on the first free layer, not an exact traction.
- Normal wall-transfer grid KE is now ledgered, not physical wall work;
  `energy_residual` remains a mixed
  diagnostic, not an energy closure.
- The same truncation exists at the pellet surface in `rigid::couple_grid`;
  nodes one cell outside a coupled pellet still lack the coupling traction.
- All fixtures that touch a wall change numerically, in the direction of
  more wall support and more friction (`0.05 s`, `h = 2 mm`, `dt = 5e-5`,
  seed 7, zero rejected steps, momentum residual `< 1e-16` before and after,
  final scheme): `slump` height `9.588 -> 9.738 mm`, spread
  `10.69 -> 10.53 mm`; `dam_break` (`10 x 20 x 10 mm` column in a `40 mm`
  channel) front `17.05 -> 16.79 mm`, residual column `9.04 -> 9.06 mm`;
  `coupled_patch` pellet centre `7.026 -> 7.207 mm`. The all-component
  variant had moved the dam-break front to `20.4 mm`, which was its
  tangential ghost weight at work; the Coulomb budget now brakes the front
  slightly instead. Fresh reproductions no longer match the archived
  manuscript data byte for byte; `docs/formal/` stays pinned to the pre-fix
  scheme.
